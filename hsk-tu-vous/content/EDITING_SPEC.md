# HSK AI video editing rules

This is the shared edit and caption standard for every HSK AI short. The renderer values live in `content/visual-system.json`; `src/EpisodeComposition.tsx`, `src/NarrativeVisual.tsx`, and `src/CaptionOverlay.tsx` read those values directly.

For new standalone advertisements, use `PROMO_WORKFLOW.md`, `creative-formats.json#promoPolicy` and `visual-system.json#promoPresentation` for the life-story treatment and minimalist frame. The dated lesson presentation below remains its own production path; do not force a teaching stack or a fixed lesson scene sequence onto a commercial. Keep actual voice cues, genuine product evidence and the shared review/publication rules.

## Frame and lesson layout

**Precedence (2026-11-12):** for lessons dated from `content/visual-system.json#lessonPresentation.illustratedFormatFromDate`, the [illustrated-glass lesson format](#illustrated-glass-lesson-format) is the active look and overrides any conflicting layout bullet in this section. Its illustrated background, one stable liquid-glass teaching card and one timed subtitle are the only authored text focuses. A third focus is allowed only when that specific explanation needs it. The rest of this section applies to earlier action lessons and legacy layouts; the cue-meaning, phone-size and review rules apply to every lesson.

For new objects/actions outside the built-in library, the AI author must complete an actual declarative drawing and motion plan using `GRAPHIC_SCENES.md` before voice production. Never substitute an unrelated object or an empty background. Follow the visible-state coverage/date and hold limit in `visual-system.json#lessonPresentation`; changing planning text alone is insufficient. Inspect the drawing's poses, transitions and fit to the actual narration. If the contract cannot depict an idea accurately, repair the asset/renderer before delivery.

The owner approved the reusable-action presentation as the default for subsequent HSK lessons. The active style and effective date are in `content/visual-system.json#lessonPresentation`. Plan a visible event for each teaching change before voice production: what object/quantity/form is shown, what changes and which exact spoken cue triggers it. Use `EPISODE_SCHEMA.md`'s `explain-actions-v1` payload instead of episode-specific renderer branches. A different camera framing alone is not evidence that a new meaning was illustrated. The coverage gate runs before voice and rendering; exact imported cues and real MP4 inspection remain mandatory. Preserve approved/published historical snapshots. **Owner decision 2026-11-09:** action lessons without lesson art render in the `content/visual-system.json#referenceGlass` look (one saturated brand gradient per scene, grain, glass chip, white kinetic subtitles). Lessons dated from `lessonPresentation.illustratedFormatFromDate` use the [illustrated-glass lesson format](#illustrated-glass-lesson-format) instead.
**Owner-confirmed frame-intent acceptance (2026-11-05):** Script each important spoken idea into a precise recognizable subject/action/change, not merely a decorative transition. Before TTS, document what must be visible at the spoken anchor and how a phone viewer will recognize it. Render and examine before/cue/settled frames against the actual localized voice. For abstract grammar prefer a truthful word-order/state/causal diagram; use an original custom SVG or, if required, a provenance-checked illustration rather than forcing an irrelevant character. Keep Hanzi/pinyin in one cue-synced subtitle block, not a second headline. For new/unapproved videos only; historical approvals remain immutable. Agent repair is root-cause based (maximum two same-method targeted attempts), and exact final preview still requires owner approval.

- Use the production canvas specified by the active renderer/global rules and the safe margins from `content/visual-system.json`. Keep all important text within those safe areas.
- Use the canonical colors in `content/visual-system.json`, with provenance in `content/reference-sources.md`. Place the HSK AI logo below the top app-overlay zone. The central stage is a transparent layout boundary, not a visible enclosing card or backplate. Use HeroUI only for small interface details inside the video, and use Pinterest/posts.design as creative references. Do not copy a reference's artwork, people, logos or exact composition; the owner-supplied reference reels define only the visual language of the [illustrated-glass lesson format](#illustrated-glass-lesson-format).
- Use one large central stage as the only content focus. Keep relevant context graphics with the active subtitle; never put a second illustration panel above it. Remove generic header chips, `ON AIR` labels/meters, and segmented progress rails. Keep only text and visuals that teach the spoken idea or perform the CTA.
- Show one teaching beat at a time. All new lessons covered by the active presentation policy follow the single-subtitle rule below. Earlier legacy, approved or published compositions retain their historical layouts.
- **One frame, one subtitle, relevant details** (action lessons before `illustratedFormatFromDate`; the illustrated-glass format has one stable teaching card and one subtitle): reusable action scenes have no headline at all, including nonduplicate headings such as “ДВА ЧАЯ: КАК СКАЗАТЬ?” and “КОЛИЧЕСТВО → ЧАШКА → ЧАЙ”. Render one narration-synced subtitle block and only meaning-linked graphic details (objects, quantities, positions or comparison diagrams). Do not place a separate Chinese/pinyin/Russian-meaning stack above another caption. Spoken Mandarin and its verified pinyin share the same subtitle block; the Russian meaning appears on its own spoken cue. A comparison may include its two authored constructions inline in that same subtitle block, with the relevant units highlighted. Keep complete speech and original word timings in the burned-in subtitles, review data and SRT. Numeric labels inside a relevant graphic and the small brand mark are details, not additional lesson headings.
- For legacy semantic layouts, an abstract semantic shot with no actors or objects uses the central teaching composition, without an empty scenery panel. Keep its authored headline, Hanzi, pinyin and meaning readable; split an authored two-part contrast into two rows. When the narration contains an action, object, change of decision or contrast, the text-only layout is insufficient: show that meaning with a contextual motion-design beat on the verified spoken cue. Use `content/visual-system.json#semanticAbstractLayout` for abstract text and `#narratedMotionLayout` for narrated motion scenes.
- Cut to the narration. For each named person, place, or action, set a matching `visualFocus` and exact imported word in `syncCueText`. Use the configurable beat-entry lead from `content/visual-system.json` so the new visual is readable as the cue begins. Use a teacher, older person, shopkeeper, or group only when the narration supports it.
- Do not hold one lesson composition through a multi-idea voice line. Use distinct semantic beats for important words: show the named object when it is spoken (such as a book or coffee), show the matching possession/absence visual on 有/没有, and return to the full phrase for the reading hold. Bind each beat to its exact imported cue; skip rapid adjacent cues that do not carry a new visual idea.
- Motion design must communicate the spoken meaning, not just animate a title or keep decorations moving. For an owner-requested narrated motion treatment, author scene `motionBeats` before rendering: a purchase transfers the relevant object, a changed decision visibly stops/reverses the intention, and a positional grammar explanation moves/highlights the matching word block. Compile every change from actual imported cue timestamps with `scripts/motion-design-plan.mjs`; a missing, repeated or out-of-order cue must block rendering. Keep ambient movement restrained and preserve reading holds.
- Move between lesson beats with the configured crossfade or short eased slide from `content/visual-system.json`. Keep the outgoing and incoming frames readable during the transition; avoid sharp wipes and instantaneous vanish/reappear changes.
- Choose caption styles through `captionStyleId`, one style at a time. Use ink text without a subtitle backing shape. In reusable action scenes, `screenCaption` and authored titles are planning metadata and must not create an extra visible text layer. Legacy styles can retain two or three treatments.
- Vary the relevant graphic action across the episode: animated objects, quantities, positions or comparisons. Keep the selected subtitle treatment consistent in new action lessons; legacy layouts can retain their historical UI treatments. Animate from Remotion frames and never leave a context illustration as a separate static picture panel.
- Use HeroUI React v3 MCP as a reference for small interface details inside the video—such as a comment field, send control, comment row, or status badge. Do not redesign the project's own management UI or add HeroUI as a Remotion runtime dependency. Adapt useful details to HSK AI styling, then animate them with Remotion frames. Use posts.design as the owner's motion reference site; turn broad motion cues into original transitions rather than copying artwork or layouts.

## Illustrated-glass lesson format

**Owner-approved 2026-11-09; refined 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-14** after review of the rendered lesson and motion reference; mandatory for lessons dated from `content/visual-system.json#lessonPresentation.illustratedFormatFromDate` (enforced by `scripts/lesson-presentation-policy.mjs`). Earlier and published lessons keep their historical look. Voice (one take per language) and the CTA scene are unchanged.

1. **Real scenes, not flat drawings.** Every lesson beat shows generated, text-free background art (≥1500 px, 2K preferred, stored as high-quality JPEG) (`visual.illustration`, payload in `EPISODE_SCHEMA.md`): `scene` (one full-frame illustration with subject anchors), `pair` (two contextual image cards only when the comparison needs them), or `board` (background art for an abstract relation; no separate formula text panel). Frames are listed in `content/lesson-art/<episode-id>.json`, made by Codex (`WORKFLOW.md` §2.6) and reused by every language.
2. **Only the HSK AI cast.** When a frame needs a character it is one of the five HSK AI characters in `content/characters/hsk-ai-characters.json` (panda = main coach and the learner's own voice, crane = tall/precise grammar, dragon = energy, monkey = drills/dialogue, rabbit = memory/warnings). They are rendered from the app's exact vector source on top of the art (`visual.illustration.cast`, with a mood per beat), so they stay sharp and never change shape. Never draw a human as a character, never invent or redraw a mascot, never bake a character into generated art.
3. **No people.** Generated art is text-free background without people or characters; an unavoidable distant figure shows no recognizable face (`scripts/generate-lesson-art.mjs` appends this rule to every prompt). Inspect each image before use.
4. **Motion follows speech.** Each meaningful visual change is bound to its exact spoken cue. Keep the subject, action and market context relevant to the narration. When a beat carries both `visual.illustration` and `visual.graphic`, compose the cue-timed transparent graphic details over the same reviewed scene/board art so an idea can move inside the frame. Reveal related objects, connector lines, highlights and count markers in a short, readable sequence; keep the real scene visible beneath them. Avoid decorative tags and labels; any overlay must explain the same spoken idea. Let the incoming scene art appear before its first spoken cue, then dissolve into it; never expose the renderer's empty black fallback between scenes. Use a short eased dissolve between scene art and beat art; lessons under the [professional motion system](#professional-motion-system) use its scene and beat transitions instead.
5. **Two text focuses.** Show one liquid-glass teaching card and one timed subtitle. The card holds the current target phrase, its pinyin and concise meaning; keep it unchanged across word-level beats and repeated/paraphrased cues. Update only when the lesson introduces a distinct target form. A contrast may add a second row in the same card; keep the content to two rows maximum. The subtitle remains visible and follows the spoken words even when it repeats the card. Do not add a headline. Add a third authored text focus only when the explanation truly needs it. Under the [professional motion system](#professional-motion-system) a motion slide replaces the card on its beats, so the frame still shows one teaching focus plus the subtitle; its formula slide is the only allowed formula board.
6. **Safe zone and subtitle placement.** Keep the small fixed HSK AI logo and wordmark, glass card and subtitle inside the Reels/Shorts safe band (`scripts/illustrated-layout.mjs`). The subtitle prefers the bottom of that band and moves up or sideways only when it would cover the main subject or a necessary comparison image.
7. **Look.** Use one restrained liquid-glass teaching card with a refracted, blurred backdrop and specular rim, brand accents, dark gradients for legibility, and clean sans-serif type. Give the small HSK AI logo and wordmark a matching glass treatment, including the standalone wordmark in the closing. Keep the teaching card stable instead of animating or replacing it on every word; under the professional motion system it assembles only when a new distinct form is spoken. Copy the references' visual language only — never their people, logos or assets.
8. **Review.** Render locally, extract cue-time frames, and check: faces never covered, no repeated text, every change matches its spoken word, nothing outside the safe band. The owner approves the exact preview before any publication.
9. **Variety, not one template.** Do not keep one background, one character pair or one comparison layout. Before writing the lesson-art manifest run `node scripts/visual-variety-plan.mjs <episode-id>`: it picks a setting not used in recent lessons (from the active A/B arm), a supporting HSK AI character by the script's role and a layout pattern (`content/visual-variety.json`); record its output as the manifest's `variety`. A/B results are read only from fixed-age (7d) Zernio observations (`--results`); below the minimum sample per arm there is no winner, and promoting a winner is an owner decision.
10. **Frames are reviewed before render.** `node scripts/review-lesson-art.mjs <episode-id>` checks every frame automatically (JPEG, resolution, aspect, not blank, provenance hash and current prompt, beats' images, cast and a free subtitle slot) and requires a visual confirmation bound to each frame's hash after looking at it (stored in the committed `public/lesson-art/<id>/provenance.json`) (text-free, no people, no baked character, matches prompt, continuity, professional quality). `content:make` and `prepare_today_video` stop with `lesson-art-required` (and the Codex brief) **before paid voice** until the review passes; `render-episode` and the TikTok render refuse an illustrated lesson without a passing review.

## Professional motion system

**Owner decision 2026-11-16, extended with the owner's reference shorts on 2026-11-17:** lessons must look professionally directed and edited. It applies to illustrated-glass `explain-actions-v1` lessons dated from `content/visual-system.json#proMotion.effectiveFromDate` while `proMotion.enabled` is true (`scripts/pro-motion-plan.mjs#proMotionActive`). Earlier lessons keep their historical look. Every number below lives only in `content/visual-system.json#proMotion`; every change still starts on its verified spoken cue, and the voice, CTA copy, cast and approval rules are unchanged.

1. **Camera.** A damped-spring camera settles on the same subtitle-safe pose as the illustrated format (`scripts/illustrated-layout.mjs#cameraFor`). A new subject whips with a short anticipation and directional motion blur; wide-to-close glides; the same subject punches in slightly; held shots push in and drift slowly. Different art cross-zooms.
2. **Shot language: art alternates with motion slides.** After the first beat establishes a scene's art, `planVisualModes` sends the lesson focus, a contrast, a full sentence or a wrong form to a clean motion slide (`src/pro-motion/MotionSlide.tsx`): a hero form, a pair or contrast of glass cards, a stacked list, or a formula of word chips. A board scene is told in slides from its first beat. Art and slides blur-dissolve; a new slide's content replaces the old one with a short blur exit. Each scene gets one slide theme (`planSlideThemes`), never the same one twice in a row; a scene with a wrong form uses the warm red theme.
3. **Meaning, not only Hanzi.** Every slide shows what the Chinese means with moving, topic-matched details, chosen automatically from the reusable lexicon `content/meaning-visuals.json` (`scripts/meaning-visual-plan.mjs`). A formula gets the animated stage of its sentence pattern: a comparison measured on one baseline (values count up from zero, bars grow or mercury rises; 比 — the gap is measured and the winner wears the adjective; 没有 — the other side wins; 跟…一样 — both stand level), an object landing on/under/in another (有 / 在) or a bare place phrase whose spot pulses (桌子上), two options weighed (还是 / 或者) or shown side by side (茶 / 咖啡), a skill or permission checked or refused (会 / 能 / 不能), inclusion (也 / 都 / 是), a degree gauge or price change (有点儿 / 一点儿), a count, a rolling price or a stated price next to the priced thing (几 / 多少 / 十五块), a wish or liking (想 / 要 / 喜欢), a question (incl. 几点), or an action with its time, partner (和…一起), refusal (不 / 没) or completion (了 / 过). A word card, hero word or stacked row gets its animated icon, or the icon leading its pattern. Person pronouns are always HSK AI cast members. When the library has no fitting component (owner 2026-11-17), Codex builds it — a lexicon entry, a new original animated icon or a new sentence-pattern stage — and adds it to the library so every later lesson reuses it: `content:make`/`prepare_today_video` stop with `meaning-visuals-required` and the brief `review/<id>/meaning-visuals-codex-brief.md` before paid voice, and render refuses an uncovered slide (procedure: `WORKFLOW.md` runbook). Values in a stage are illustrative and follow the sentence's meaning.
4. **One focus per frame.** A slide shows one idea. Its elements blur in one after another; a formula's word chips light up when the narration names them, and the other chips dim. A wrong form is struck through and stamped with a red ✕ on its cue. On slide beats the teaching card and graphic overlay step aside.
5. **Kinetic card.** On art beats the glass card assembles a new form character by character, keeps the lesson focus (for example 比) in gold, and moves earlier rows instead of cutting them. It does not move on repeated or word-level cues.
6. **Karaoke subtitles.** The timed subtitle shows the whole page at once; the word being spoken lifts slightly, words still to come wait dimmed. Word starts come only from the imported word cues (a multi-word cue gives all its words its own start); when the displayed words differ from the cue words, the subtitle falls back to plain words. On a slide the subtitle takes the slide slot at the bottom of the safe band, and a pure Chinese line the slide already shows large is not repeated.
7. **Scene transitions.** Scenes change with a varied zoom-through, diagonal wipe with a soft gold edge, vertical whip or warm light leak (`planSceneTransitions`, never the same twice in a row; the CTA always enters with the light leak). The outgoing scene stays visible behind the incoming art; never show an empty black frame.
8. **Cast and graphics.** The HSK AI character springs in, reacts with a small hop when its mood changes, and breathes and sways while idle. Graphic nodes that stay are kept, moved ones morph, new strokes draw on and removed ones fade out.
9. **Finish.** Lesson frames get a light vignette and fine moving grain; the CTA keeps its own look.
10. **Sound design.** The official mix uses original effects synthesized in this repository (`scripts/build-pro-sfx.mjs` → `public/sfx/pro/`, hash-listed in its `manifest.json`): whooshes on scene transitions and on whips or art/slide switches, a pop on a new card form, an impact on a wrong-form stamp and a chime on the first positive reaction of a scene (`planProSfx`). There is no opening cue, effects keep the minimum gaps in `proMotion.sound` and stay below the narration, and they replace the legacy soft cut/taps rather than doubling them. When a SoundKit P4 test plan is present, these effects are off. The final mix still passes the loudness/true-peak check in `content/WORKFLOW.md` and the owner's exact preview approval.
11. **Still forbidden:** flash, strobe, glitch, repeated bounce, per-word pulsing, people or copied reference assets. A slide's light sweep and glow stay soft, and nothing animates when no new idea is spoken.

## Video cover

Every new video review package includes a dedicated vertical cover. It should make the topic recognizable before playback, including when cropped in a profile grid. Use one short title in the video's language and keep the title, teaching labels, and main subject within the central crop. Skip subtitles, pinyin, CTAs, hashtags, and any second headline.

For lessons and series parts, use the episode's own setting and meaningful objects. Show the exact Hanzi taught in the lesson on one or two Liquid Glass labels; use a paired layout only when a real comparison benefits from it. Give the original HSK AI panda an expression that fits the scene, using the app's exact vector and a registered pose. Keep its face and the lesson objects visible. For advertisements, use the actual product or story focal point; use a character only if it belongs to that story. Never use a CTA frame, unrelated stock art, or fabricated product UI as a cover.

Reuse reviewed lesson or promo art when available. When the subject exists only in the video renderer, use a frame from the relevant teaching or story scene as source art. Check the exported JPEG at phone and profile-grid sizes for readable text, recognizable objects and unclipped faces. The visual design is generated from each episode's content, so another lesson must not inherit the tea-and-coffee artwork or text.

For new action lessons, storyboard and creative-format selection are planning context only: they must not restore the old headline, separate illustration panel or stacked teaching text. Legacy layout tokens live in `content/visual-system.json#storyboardLayout` and creative packaging in `content/creative-formats.json`. Inspect real rendered frames and verify the approved voice + SRT/VTT export.

The following describe the retained legacy cinematic renderer, not defaults for new action lessons:

## Per-video component selection (MotionKit pilot)

The owner requested an extensive reusable source/reference collection, but **every new video selects a small, content-specific combination**—the whole collection is never rendered at once. **The creative selector is the Codex video-production AI, not a new remote AI API.** The existing deterministic code only enumerates compatible candidates and checks constraints; Codex must independently choose and document the actual per-scene combination in a marked `TEST_ONLY` decision before optional rendering. Favor visual meaning, speech synchrony and one dominant focus over variety for variety's sake. Avoid repeating the same complete combination across neighboring videos where an equally accurate distinct treatment exists. Preserve the active illustrated-glass format: no extra teaching cards, second timed subtitle, generic dashboards or synthetic product UI. Any applied art treatment must bind to real imported narration word cues and the current approved-voice audio hash. Free component catalogs/skills are only references until individually adapted as deterministic Remotion-frame elements. Paid/Pro sources require licensed access.

The implementation is opt-in and experimental until real MP4/phone-size review and owner sign-off. `src/video-components/README.md` documents the current guard and exact review flag; `content/visual-system.json` remains the only numeric design-token authority. Nothing about this policy overrides the canonical CTA, character art, source screenshots, narration, approval or publication rules.

## Motion and effects

| Moment        | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First hook    | Start with the promised subject already visible. Use a calm reveal or short eased slide; no flash, pulse, or opening sound cue.                                                                                 |
| Scene change  | Ease into the new lesson composition with a short crossfade. Use one soft cut cue after the opening scene; keep it under the narration. Professional-motion lessons use the varied transitions and effects in [Professional motion system](#professional-motion-system).                                                                          |
| Teaching beat | Start one lead action on the matching spoken cue, then stage its relevant details in the same composition with short eased reveals. Keep movement readable, let the elements settle before the next idea, and do not rely on a background cut alone when an authored detail can move. |
| CTA           | Follow the currently approved CTA in `content/cta-lab.json` and its visual sequence in `content/CTA_LAB.md`. Match each visual action to its spoken cue. |
| Reference motion | Use posts.design motion references only when relevant to the current approved episode/CTA. Do not resurrect previously superseded comment/DM UI or reproduce another author's artwork. |

- Use one lead movement per beat, with a small sequence of related detail movements when the authored visual supports them. The spoken word activates the matching caption and context cue. Keep ambient motion slow inside the centered open stage; do not add flash, strobe, bright sweep, glitch, or per-word pulsing. The [professional motion system](#professional-motion-system) defines its own camera, slide and karaoke motion.
- For a visible cardinal counter or quantity total, begin at `0` on the exact spoken cue, ease to the authored target using `content/visual-system.json#motion.numericCountFrames`, and stop on that exact value. Animate its matching dots or progress line in sync, then hold the total for reading. Do not animate dates, cue text, ordinal position labels, or unrelated numbers as counters.
- CTA uses one large open central composition. Read the current offer and action sequence from `content/cta-lab.json` and `content/CTA_LAB.md`. Change each visual on the matching spoken cue and highlight the relevant action. Do not stage real taps or claim the app was downloaded. Keep the full spoken CTA in the timed captions instead of repeating it in a headline chip.
- Use two or three CTA transition treatments when their beat timing supports them: type-reveal for the HSK AI intro, slide-match for the profile and website screens, and mask-reveal for the profile-link overlay. Keep every transition driven by Remotion frames and shared tokens in content/visual-system.json; never reuse source footage, artwork, or an exact layout.
- For Instagram and YouTube, use the owner-supplied screenshots required by the current CTA Lab and time them to the spoken action words. Facebook uses a separate cue-synced, UI-free ending. For TikTok's approved AI-install-help CTA on all new video types, use cue-synced kinetic typography and eased type-reveal, slide-match, or mask-reveal effects; keep hskai.app readable and never simulate a third-party AI answer or completed installation. Telegram receives no video.
- Do not use flash or strobe anywhere in the video, including the opening hook. Avoid bright sweep overlays, rapid zoom, glitch, and repeated bounce; use eased opacity and small positional changes for smooth motion. Professional-motion lessons may use their spring camera, whip pans with motion blur and zoom transitions, within its tokens.
- Use a quiet instrumental bed under the narration when the episode has a resolved music asset. Use the music volume and fades from `content/visual-system.json`; keep speech clearly dominant. Do not use SFX as a music substitute. Use the generated soft cut only at scene changes after the hook and at most two quiet soft taps per scene for meaningful teaching transitions. Professional-motion lessons use their own cue-bound effects instead (item 10 of [Professional motion system](#professional-motion-system)). Keep effects below the narration; do not add a cue to every word or decorative beat.
- **SoundKit P2 is advisory only:** For a new video Codex reads the per-language `soundKitTest` brief, reviews actual sound candidates or chooses `SILENCE`, and records source-linked reasons according to `content/sound-design.json` (the only SoundKit policy owner). P2/P3 decisions do **not** authorize any SoundKit library sound in the official final mix (the original professional-motion effects are a separate, owner-requested part of that mix). The separate P4 `HSK_AI_SOUND_DESIGN_TEST=1` preview may audition chosen verified voice-cue-bound SFX in an isolated test MP4; it suppresses legacy soft-cut/tap ONLY in that preview to prevent doubling. An actual modified official MP4 remains prohibited until P5 real LUFS/true-peak/clipping + speaker/headphone QA and P6 explicit owner acceptance. Existing mix tokens, soft-cut/tap limits and approved narration remain unchanged in `content/visual-system.json#sound`. Do not turn a static product screenshot into a fictional click, payment or successful operation; no new AI audio API is allowed. See `content/WORKFLOW.md#codex-soundkit-p2--test-only-automatic-localized-source-briefs-no-audio-mix`.
- Drive all animation from Remotion frames. Do not use CSS time-based transitions or animations.

## Russian captions

- Use the exact narration and Gemini Transcribe word timings produced through the approved provider in `content/voice-profile.json`. The import step must confirm a normalized exact-token match (or the bounded timed near-miss substitution in `AI_RULES.md`, owner 2026-11-23); do not move timings by hand to fit a changed script.
- Group adjacent word cues into readable phrases using the current limits and hold timings in `content/visual-system.json`. Never stretch captions across unrelated speech.
- Keep captions as high-contrast ink text without backing shapes. Use the caption safe areas, variants and positions from `content/visual-system.json`, including the CTA-specific placement to avoid covering screenshots.
- Keep one or two short lines on screen at a time. Do not cover Hanzi, pinyin, Russian meaning, the HSK AI logo, or the final CTA. If the available timed cues cannot fit clearly, stop and ask for a cleaner export; do not shrink text below the configured size.
- Use the same complete scene-bounded spoken phrase pages for the MP4 and `captions.srt`. Reusable action scenes can add verified tone-marked pinyin inline in that same subtitle block while Mandarin is spoken; never add a second transcript layer. Each scene's style range follows imported audio timestamps. A script edit requires a fresh matching MP3 and word-timed caption export before rendering.

## Review before delivery

This is a pass/fail gate for every new package and every revised, unapproved version. Keep approved/published historical versions immutable unless the owner explicitly requests a new version. A valid JSON plan, passing hold/cue checks, or a polished title does not count as visual approval.

- **Cue-to-frame check:** play the final MP4 with its real narration. For every authored action beat, inspect the frame before the cue, the first frame at its imported word anchor, the visible movement, and its settled state. The spoken object/action must be the thing that appears or changes; camera movement, color changes, or decorative bobbing alone do not count. If the frame arrives late, depicts a different action, or remains unchanged through a meaning change, fix the drawing/timing and render again.
- **Recognition check:** pause on the frame and name the real object, place, person, or action without using an extra headline to explain it. The subtitle may teach the language, but it must not rescue an unrelated, generic, or unrecognizable picture. Draw enough identifying shape and context; make the subject large enough to read. For an abstract grammar point, show the actual relationship between the spoken words instead of an unrelated scene.
- **Focus and text check:** apply “One frame, one subtitle, relevant details” above. Keep one dominant subject/action and one subtitle block; remove repeated narration, a second headline, a duplicate translation, and decorative labels. Supporting details must explain the same spoken idea and must not compete with its focal subject.
- **Comparison check:** when a line contrasts two situations, keep the same visual grammar on both sides and make the real difference immediately visible. Do not use an unexplained color swap, a giant X, or generic boxes in place of the object/state being compared.
- **Phone-size check:** inspect a still or contact sheet at 270×480 (one quarter of the 1080×1920 frame). The hero, its action/state, and the single subtitle must still be distinct. If they merge, shrink into an icon, clip, or leave the stage looking empty, simplify or enlarge the meaningful art and repeat the render.
- **Detail-motion check:** for each graphic overlay, inspect the pre-cue pose, first cue frame, mid-move pose, and settled pose in the real render. Confirm the overlay stays aligned with the reviewed background, completes before the next idea, and keeps counters at zero before the cue and at the exact target after the count-up.

Watch the full video once with sound, then review the cue-frame stills/contact sheet against the narration and `motion-timeline.json`. Any failed item means the package is not ready: repair the art, staging, motion, or timing and render/review it again. For retained legacy cinematic scenes, also inspect context motion and teaching text against their storyboard tokens. The 50-second target in `content/visual-system.json#duration` is a pacing reference only; there is no minimum or fixed maximum duration. Render all natural speech and the complete CTA; never stretch audio, add silence, truncate narration or edit spoken content just to reach a target duration. A visual, caption, or sound edit invalidates the previous MP4 and package approval; render and review the new version before approval.
