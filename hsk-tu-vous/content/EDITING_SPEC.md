# HSK AI video editing rules

This is the shared edit and caption standard for every HSK AI short. The renderer values live in `content/visual-system.json`; `src/EpisodeComposition.tsx`, `src/NarrativeVisual.tsx`, and `src/CaptionOverlay.tsx` read those values directly.

For new standalone advertisements, use `PROMO_WORKFLOW.md`, `creative-formats.json#promoPolicy` and `visual-system.json#promoPresentation` for the life-story treatment and minimalist frame. The dated lesson presentation below remains its own production path; do not force a teaching stack or a fixed lesson scene sequence onto a commercial. Keep actual voice cues, genuine product evidence and the shared review/publication rules.

## Frame and lesson layout

For new objects/actions outside the built-in library, the AI author must complete an actual declarative drawing and motion plan using `GRAPHIC_SCENES.md` before voice production. Never substitute an unrelated object or an empty background. Follow the visible-state coverage/date and hold limit in `visual-system.json#lessonPresentation`; changing planning text alone is insufficient. Inspect the drawing's poses, transitions and fit to the actual narration. If the contract cannot depict an idea accurately, repair the asset/renderer before delivery.

The owner approved the reusable-action presentation as the default for subsequent HSK lessons. The active style and effective date are in `content/visual-system.json#lessonPresentation`. Plan a visible event for each teaching change before voice production: what object/quantity/form is shown, what changes and which exact spoken cue triggers it. Use `EPISODE_SCHEMA.md`'s `explain-actions-v1` payload instead of episode-specific renderer branches. A different camera framing alone is not evidence that a new meaning was illustrated. The coverage gate runs before voice and rendering; exact imported cues and real MP4 inspection remain mandatory. Preserve approved/published historical snapshots.

- Use the production canvas specified by the active renderer/global rules and the safe margins from `content/visual-system.json`. Keep all important text within those safe areas.
- Use the canonical colors in `content/visual-system.json`, with provenance in `content/reference-sources.md`. Place the HSK AI logo below the top app-overlay zone. The central stage is a transparent layout boundary, not a visible enclosing card or backplate. Use HeroUI only for small interface details inside the video, and use Pinterest/posts.design as creative references. Do not copy a reference's artwork or exact composition.
- Use one large central stage as the only content focus. Keep relevant context graphics with the active subtitle; never put a second illustration panel above it. Remove generic header chips, `ON AIR` labels/meters, and segmented progress rails. Keep only text and visuals that teach the spoken idea or perform the CTA.
- Show one teaching beat at a time. All new lessons covered by the active presentation policy follow the single-subtitle rule below. Earlier legacy, approved or published compositions retain their historical layouts.
- **One frame, one subtitle, relevant details:** reusable action scenes have no headline at all, including nonduplicate headings such as “ДВА ЧАЯ: КАК СКАЗАТЬ?” and “КОЛИЧЕСТВО → ЧАШКА → ЧАЙ”. Render one narration-synced subtitle block and only meaning-linked graphic details (objects, quantities, positions or comparison diagrams). Do not place a separate Chinese/pinyin/Russian-meaning stack above another caption. Spoken Mandarin and its verified pinyin share the same subtitle block; the Russian meaning appears on its own spoken cue. A comparison may include its two authored constructions inline in that same subtitle block, with the relevant units highlighted. Keep complete speech and original word timings in the burned-in subtitles, review data and SRT. Numeric labels inside a relevant graphic and the small brand mark are details, not additional lesson headings.
- For legacy semantic layouts, an abstract semantic shot with no actors or objects uses the central teaching composition, without an empty scenery panel. Keep its authored headline, Hanzi, pinyin and meaning readable; split an authored two-part contrast into two rows. When the narration contains an action, object, change of decision or contrast, the text-only layout is insufficient: show that meaning with a contextual motion-design beat on the verified spoken cue. Use `content/visual-system.json#semanticAbstractLayout` for abstract text and `#narratedMotionLayout` for narrated motion scenes.
- Cut to the narration. For each named person, place, or action, set a matching `visualFocus` and exact imported word in `syncCueText`. Use the configurable beat-entry lead from `content/visual-system.json` so the new visual is readable as the cue begins. Use a teacher, older person, shopkeeper, or group only when the narration supports it.
- Do not hold one lesson composition through a multi-idea voice line. Use distinct semantic beats for important words: show the named object when it is spoken (such as a book or coffee), show the matching possession/absence visual on 有/没有, and return to the full phrase for the reading hold. Bind each beat to its exact imported cue; skip rapid adjacent cues that do not carry a new visual idea.
- Motion design must communicate the spoken meaning, not just animate a title or keep decorations moving. For an owner-requested narrated motion treatment, author scene `motionBeats` before rendering: a purchase transfers the relevant object, a changed decision visibly stops/reverses the intention, and a positional grammar explanation moves/highlights the matching word block. Compile every change from actual imported cue timestamps with `scripts/motion-design-plan.mjs`; a missing, repeated or out-of-order cue must block rendering. Keep ambient movement restrained and preserve reading holds.
- Move between lesson beats with the configured crossfade or short eased slide from `content/visual-system.json`. Keep the outgoing and incoming frames readable during the transition; avoid sharp wipes and instantaneous vanish/reappear changes.
- Choose caption styles through `captionStyleId`, one style at a time. Use ink text without a subtitle backing shape. In reusable action scenes, `screenCaption` and authored titles are planning metadata and must not create an extra visible text layer. Legacy styles can retain two or three treatments.
- Vary the relevant graphic action across the episode: animated objects, quantities, positions or comparisons. Keep the selected subtitle treatment consistent in new action lessons; legacy layouts can retain their historical UI treatments. Animate from Remotion frames and never leave a context illustration as a separate static picture panel.
- Use HeroUI React v3 MCP as a reference for small interface details inside the video—such as a comment field, send control, comment row, or status badge. Do not redesign the project's own management UI or add HeroUI as a Remotion runtime dependency. Adapt useful details to HSK AI styling, then animate them with Remotion frames. Use posts.design as the owner's motion reference site; turn broad motion cues into original transitions rather than copying artwork or layouts.

## Semantic actions (P0/P1 foundation only)

The proposed semantic shot/action vocabulary and strict cue-time contract are defined in `content/SEMANTIC_VISUAL_SCHEMA.md`. P0/P1 validate cue timing, P2 supplies Remotion action visuals, and a connected auto-review workflow now produces **review MP4s** for eligible clean future drafts using verified Gemini word cues. A socially publishable semantic MP4 is **not** yet authorized. Distinguish composition appearing before a cue (permitted) from movement that *depicts* the spoken verb (must begin on the confirmed cue frame, not on the early storyboard beat lead). Reject unmatched/ambiguous cues; do not approximate them by equal scene division. Do not apply to CTA, prior approvals or published episodes.

### P3 semantic shot recommendations

A read-only adviser may propose scene-specific environments, framing and meaningful movements backed by actual authored narration; see `content/SEMANTIC_SHOT_ADVISER.md`. Visual variety does not justify random gestures. A gesture missing from that legacy adviser must be flagged rather than faked; new action lessons can author it through `GRAPHIC_SCENES.md` when the drawing accurately depicts the meaning. Do not apply an advised `syncCueText` as verified until it matches imported Gemini word timestamps. This planning does not alter production rules or render approval.

## Legacy cinematic storyboard scenes

Earlier lessons outside the active presentation policy can retain their `scene.storyboard` treatment with an illustrated location, Hanzi/pinyin/meaning stage and existing word-timed captions. For new action lessons, storyboard and creative-format selection are planning context only: they must not restore the old headline, separate illustration panel or stacked teaching text. The action stage and single subtitle remain the visible presentation. Animate only from Remotion frames. Do not use storyboard on CTA scenes or rewrite approved/published snapshots. Legacy layout tokens live in `content/visual-system.json#storyboardLayout` and creative packaging in `content/creative-formats.json`. Inspect real rendered frames and verify the approved voice + SRT/VTT export.

The following describe the retained legacy cinematic renderer, not defaults for new action lessons:

- Make the illustrated frame feel alive with gentle camera drift, character bob/sway and narration-relevant gestures. Combine one dominant teaching action with restrained ambient movement, keeping the words readable. Read motion amounts from `content/visual-system.json#storyboardLayout`.
- Give Chinese teaching text a short, decaying entrance quiver and a settled reading hold. Use the canonical `heroQuiverPx` and `heroQuiverDeg` tokens. When a beat uses a drawing or text reveal, let the reveal unfold visibly in time with its spoken cue before holding the completed form.
- Keep 在 and all other Hanzi compact and lower in the teaching composition, with pinyin and Russian meaning grouped below. The active `hanziScale` and `heroTopPx` tokens in `content/visual-system.json#storyboardLayout` own their size and vertical position; apply them consistently to every Chinese teaching beat. Preserve caption separation and phone readability.

## Motion and effects

| Moment        | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First hook    | Start with the promised subject already visible. Use a calm reveal or short eased slide; no flash, pulse, or opening sound cue.                                                                                 |
| Scene change  | Ease into the new lesson composition with a short crossfade. Use one soft cut cue after the opening scene; keep it under the narration.                                                                          |
| Teaching beat | Change the contextual illustration and lesson composition on the matching spoken cue. Use one short eased slide or reveal per idea change. Keep movement small and settle the frame before the next idea.       |
| CTA           | Follow the currently approved CTA in `content/cta-lab.json` and its visual sequence in `content/CTA_LAB.md`. Match each visual action to its spoken cue. |
| Reference motion | Use posts.design motion references only when relevant to the current approved episode/CTA. Do not resurrect previously superseded comment/DM UI or reproduce another author's artwork. |

- Use one main movement per beat. The spoken word activates the matching caption and context cue. Keep ambient motion slow inside the centered open stage; do not add flash, strobe, bright sweep, glitch, or per-word pulsing.
- CTA uses one large open central composition. Read the current offer and action sequence from `content/cta-lab.json` and `content/CTA_LAB.md`. Change each visual on the matching spoken cue and highlight the relevant action. Do not stage real taps or claim the app was downloaded. Keep the full spoken CTA in the timed captions instead of repeating it in a headline chip.
- Use two or three CTA transition treatments when their beat timing supports them: type-reveal for the HSK AI intro, slide-match for the profile and website screens, and mask-reveal for the profile-link overlay. Keep every transition driven by Remotion frames and shared tokens in content/visual-system.json; never reuse source footage, artwork, or an exact layout.
- For Instagram and YouTube, use the owner-supplied screenshots required by the current CTA Lab and time them to the spoken action words. For TikTok's approved manual-DM variant, use cue-synced kinetic typography and eased type-reveal, slide-match, or mask-reveal effects instead of screenshots; do not simulate a sent DM or completed download.
- Do not use flash or strobe anywhere in the video, including the opening hook. Avoid bright sweep overlays, rapid zoom, glitch, and repeated bounce; use eased opacity and small positional changes for smooth motion.
- Use a quiet instrumental bed under the narration when the episode has a resolved music asset. Use the music volume and fades from `content/visual-system.json`; keep speech clearly dominant. Do not use SFX as a music substitute. Use the generated soft cut only at scene changes after the hook and at most two quiet soft taps per scene for meaningful teaching transitions. Keep effects below the narration; do not add a cue to every word or decorative beat.
- Drive all animation from Remotion frames. Do not use CSS time-based transitions or animations.

## Russian captions

- Use the exact narration and Gemini Transcribe word timings produced through the approved provider in `content/voice-profile.json`. The import step must confirm a normalized exact-token match; do not move timings by hand to fit a changed script.
- Group adjacent word cues into readable phrases using the current limits and hold timings in `content/visual-system.json`. Never stretch captions across unrelated speech.
- Keep captions as high-contrast ink text without backing shapes. Use the caption safe areas, variants and positions from `content/visual-system.json`, including the CTA-specific placement to avoid covering screenshots.
- Keep one or two short lines on screen at a time. Do not cover Hanzi, pinyin, Russian meaning, the HSK AI logo, or the final CTA. If the available timed cues cannot fit clearly, stop and ask for a cleaner export; do not shrink text below the configured size.
- Use the same complete scene-bounded spoken phrase pages for the MP4 and `captions.srt`. Reusable action scenes can add verified tone-marked pinyin inline in that same subtitle block while Mandarin is spoken; never add a second transcript layer. Each scene's style range follows imported audio timestamps. A script edit requires a fresh matching MP3 and word-timed caption export before rendering.

## Review before delivery

This is a pass/fail gate for every new package and every revised, unapproved version. Keep approved/published historical versions immutable unless the owner explicitly requests a new version. A valid JSON plan, passing hold/cue checks, or a polished title does not count as visual approval.

- **Cue-to-frame check:** play the final MP4 with its real narration. For every authored action beat, inspect the frame before the cue, the first frame at its imported word anchor, the visible movement, and its settled state. The spoken object/action must be the thing that appears or changes; camera movement, color changes, or decorative bobbing alone do not count. If the frame arrives late, depicts a different action, or remains unchanged through a meaning change, fix the drawing/timing and render again.
- **Recognition check:** pause on the frame and name the real object, place, person, or action without using an extra headline to explain it. The subtitle may teach the language, but it must not rescue an unrelated, generic, or unrecognizable picture. Draw enough identifying shape and context; make the subject large enough to read. For an abstract grammar point, show the actual relationship between the spoken words instead of an unrelated scene.
- **Focus and text check:** apply “One frame, one subtitle, relevant details” above. Keep one dominant subject/action and one subtitle block; remove repeated narration, a second headline, a duplicate translation, and decorative labels. Supporting details must explain the same spoken idea and must not compete with its focal subject.
- **Comparison check:** when a line contrasts two situations, keep the same visual grammar on both sides and make the real difference immediately visible. Do not use an unexplained color swap, a giant X, or generic boxes in place of the object/state being compared.
- **Phone-size check:** inspect a still or contact sheet at 270×480 (one quarter of the 1080×1920 frame). The hero, its action/state, and the single subtitle must still be distinct. If they merge, shrink into an icon, clip, or leave the stage looking empty, simplify or enlarge the meaningful art and repeat the render.

Watch the full video once with sound, then review the cue-frame stills/contact sheet against the narration and `motion-timeline.json`. Any failed item means the package is not ready: repair the art, staging, motion, or timing and render/review it again. For retained legacy cinematic scenes, also inspect context motion and teaching text against their storyboard tokens. The 45–55 second range in `content/visual-system.json#duration` is preferred guidance, not a limit. Render all natural speech and the complete CTA; never stretch audio, add silence, truncate narration or edit spoken content just to reach a target duration. A visual, caption, or sound edit invalidates the previous MP4 and package approval; render and review the new version before approval.
