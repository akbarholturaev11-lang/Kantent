# Episode and review schema

The calendar is the index. Each calendar item points to one CTA-free planned JSON lesson in `content/episodes/` for new dates. CTA text and cue-synced visuals live **only** in the canonical CTA Lab. The assembly of an active *review copy* happens in `scripts/episode-cta-assembly.mjs` when the agent opens or creates the dated production plan. Older historical source files may retain previously registered CTA snapshots; do not rewrite published or approved history.

## Language and source identity

`content/languages.json` owns locale codes and content roots. The shared calendar points to the canonical lesson. Localized records keep the same `id` and `date`, use their own `languageCode`/`voiceProfileId`, and include `localization: {language, sourceEpisodeId, sourceHash, authoredOn, status}`. `sourceHash` is SHA-256 of `JSON.stringify` on the raw canonical lesson, calculated by `lessonSourceHash`; stale/missing bindings stop production. Do not copy imported audio, word timestamps, approval or publication records into a translation. Author translated motion cue words for its own narration; they are resolved against that language's real word timestamps.

CTA and Hook labs are selected from the same configured content root. The legacy schema key `offer.canonicalRussian` is retained for compatibility but stores that lab's language-specific canonical copy. The localized CTA source hash binds the shared offer, direction, platform templates and rule history; measured experiment/readback histories remain separate. Existing untagged source/publication history belongs to the original series.

## Semantic visual planning (v1; owner-authorized production)

A retained additive contract is defined in [SEMANTIC_VISUAL_SCHEMA.md](SEMANTIC_VISUAL_SCHEMA.md) with enumerations in `semantic-visual-system.json`. Eligible earlier working drafts outside the active presentation policy can receive semanticShot scenes and unambiguous narrator-supported actions automatically before TTS. New action lessons follow the approved default below. The semantic MP4 renderer receives exact Gemini word-anchored cue props; its earlier production rollout was owner-authorized after a real-video trial. Each exact video still requires separate final-preview approval before publishing. Central CTA remains unchanged. Existing episodes without `semanticVisual` remain unchanged.

### P3 read-only scene advice

Before choosing a future scene's `semanticShot`/`semanticAction`, agents may request *read-only* shot/gesture proposals using `scripts/semantic-shot-adviser.mjs` or `preview_semantic_shots`; the rule registry is `content/semantic-shot-intents.json`, explained in `content/SEMANTIC_SHOT_ADVISER.md`. `proposedCueText` is narration evidence **not** imported Gemini word timing. Standalone proposals never execute or edit files. A separate clean-draft auto-applicator may bind uniquely supported word cues into review shots; only real imported Gemini timings make them executable in a review MP4. No CTA or already reviewed/published episode is rewritten.

## Retained cinematic storyboard treatment

`scene.storyboard = {formatId, setting, character, mood, sign?}` remains available for legacy rendering and as creative-planning metadata in new action lessons. Allowed `formatId` values are owned by `content/creative-formats.json`, not by CTA Lab. `setting` supports `street`, `cafe`, `campus`, `home`; `character` supports `student`, `shopkeeper`; and `mood` supports `question`, `mistake`, `answer`, `practice`. For `speed-quiz`, include 2–3 short `options`. Only source-supported or harmless general context labels are allowed. The original `zai-location` pilot retains its narration, hook experiment and approved voice provider.

The authored action renderer takes priority in lessons using the approved default. In retained legacy episodes, a timeline-validated semantic scene takes priority over `CinematicStoryScene`; otherwise ordinary storyboard rendering remains available. CTA never accepts storyboard and always comes from the central CTA Lab. Illustrated styles do **not** create real footage, a verified real location, a valid TTS export, or publication approval. Production MP4 still requires approved MP3 and timed word cues, language-source checks, and exact publication preview review.

## Adaptive AI authoring and timed rendering

`npm run content:storytelling-generate -- <episode-id>` creates a **read-only source-bound brief** without paying for or contacting an AI model. To request actual Russian narration edits, configure local `GEMINI_API_KEY` and opt-in `HSK_AI_ALLOW_GEMINI_SCRIPT=true`, then invoke the same command with `--ai`. It makes **two** text-model calls (candidate and separate critical review). The model and operational settings are controlled by `creative-formats.json#storytellingPolicy.aiDraft`. This is not the fixed TTS voice model and does not modify it.

The candidate may alter only Russian narration of already-authored non-hook scenes, must preserve each scene's Chinese text runs verbatim and order, and must provide all first-payoff/re-hook/payoff cues plus their later payoff scene IDs. The code checks source receipt presence, full episode content hash, planned/unsounded/CTA-free eligibility, protected fields, in-lesson CTA leakage and structural timing. This is **not a Mandarin correctness proof**: the Gemini critic and human reviewer must recheck Chinese language references; a passing AI critic does not count as the owner's final approval. The CLI prints an unsaved proposal; never write it to the canonical calendar/source automatically.

When and only when an approved future script already has `storytelling` metadata, `auto-storyboard` assigns its authored re-hook scene the existing `question` mood and the later payoff scene the existing `answer` mood, respecting any authored storyboard. Semantic shots and the Remotion renderer remain in place. Before an opt-in semantic render, `validateStorytellingWordTiming` locates the cue phrases in **imported exact Gemini single-word caption timings**, requires a unique match, and rejects an actual first payoff later than the 30% policy boundary or a reversed question/answer. No approximate beat time may substitute for actual Gemini cue data; if the transcriber does not retain matching Hanzi tokenization, the render stops for editorial reconciliation. Changes to narration demand a new voice/timestamp/render and owner-preview approval; immutable publication/CTA records are never edited.

## Optional Adaptive Storytelling v1 (unapproved future lessons only)

For newly authored, not-yet-voiced **CTA-free** plans, `episode.storytelling` may annotate a script's existing narrated beats. It does not create a new `scene.kind` or a rendered scene and MUST NOT be injected into approved/published records. Existing records without this object remain valid.

- The only active policy is `content/creative-formats.json#storytellingPolicy` (`direct`, `single-rehook`, `layered`; no forced extra questions).
- `schemaVersion: 1`, `pattern` and `firstPayoff: {sceneId, cueText}` describe the earliest real instructional answer. `cueText` must appear verbatim in the cited teaching scene's narration, and its scene must start within the opening ratio set in the policy. Scene timing is planning evidence, NOT exact spoken-word timing.
- `reHooks: []` contains zero or more `{sceneId, cueText, payoffSceneId, payoffCueText, reason}`. Every re-hook and payoff cue must occur verbatim in its existing scene's narration. Payoff must appear later than its distinct question, and both must be after the first teaching payoff. CTA scenes are never eligible.
- `direct` requires no re-hook. `single-rehook` annotates exactly one genuine additional learner problem. `layered` permits at most two if editorially justified; this is not a mandatory eight-block script structure.
- The read-only `npm run content:storytelling-plan -- <episode-id>` returns evidence-linked candidates and explicit uncertainty. It NEVER authors prose, changes scene order, writes a JSON plan, generates Gemini audio or publishes media.
- `validateEpisode` rejects malformed opt-in metadata. No metadata is added to old drafts automatically. Hook Lab still owns the opening format and analytics, CTA Lab still owns the platform-specific closing and voice/cue timestamps still own actual synchronization.


## Canonical episode record

### Reusable narrated actions (approved default)

`motionDesignStyle:"explain-actions-v1"` selects content-driven quantity, phrase, numeral, ordinal, comparison and choice scenes. Each `motionBeats[]` entry has an exact `cueText`/optional occurrence, an `explains` teaching rationale and a `visual` payload (`title`, `hanzi`, `pinyin`, `meaning`, optional object/count/grouping, exact phrase parts/focus, number value, two choices/selection or two comparison models). Authored titles, Hanzi, pinyin and meanings remain structured planning/alignment metadata; their presentation follows `EDITING_SPEC.md`'s single-subtitle rule. The renderer contains no episode IDs or teaching examples. Supported objects and actions are validated in `scripts/action-design-plan.mjs`; numeric layout and palette values live in `visual-system.json#narratedActionLayout` and `#narratedActionPalette`.

The active policy in `visual-system.json#lessonPresentation` selects this default for new dated drafts. Author `motionDesign:true`, its selected style and complete per-scene `motionBeats` in future canonical CTA-free lessons; the loader also selects the style for newly materialized eligible drafts, but never invents teaching actions. Every lesson scene must have at least two distinct meaning-linked states. Missing visuals, unsupported quantities/objects, wrong phrase assembly and malformed cue declarations block voice and render; missing/ambiguous imported spoken cues block render. These checks establish structural coverage, not subjective quality or Chinese accuracy. Inspect actual voice, action frames, readability and transitions in the MP4. `content/pilots/quantity-cafe-20261026.json` remains the original reviewed example and `scripts/prepare-action-pilot.mjs` its historical one-off preparer. The owner's format approval enables the default, while publication still requires approval of each exact final preview. Legacy lessons/styles and approved publication history remain independent.

- `id`, `date`, `timezone`, `languageCode`: identity; ISO date and Asia/Shanghai.
- For new unapproved plans from `content/visual-system.json#duration.effectiveFromDate`, set `durationTargetSec` from that policy's `targetSec`. The target includes the centrally assembled CTA. The final duration follows verified speech timings and must meet the policy minimum; target-band deviations require explicit review. Earlier approved/published snapshots retain their original values.
- `voiceProvider`, `voiceProfileId`: production provider key and the fixed profile in the selected language’s voice-profile.json.
- `title`, `level`, `topic`, `durationTargetSec`, `caption`, `hashtags`, `cta`: packaging and learning context. **`cta` is an immutable-per-registration draft/render snapshot** resolved from the central `content/cta-lab.json`, NOT a second editable CTA policy. `audienceProblem` names one concrete beginner pain point; `learningOutcome` states the single useful result the viewer should get from the lesson.
- `learningFacts`, `examples`: the intended lesson point and Chinese examples. These are planning notes until checked.
- `learningFactSources`: evidence receipts, one per source: `{ title, url, checkedAt, supports }`. `checkedAt` is YYYY-MM-DD and `supports` names the specific fact(s) the source supports. Empty means language-source review has not been recorded.
- `hookExperiment`: required preregistration `{ id, formatId, hypothesis, primaryMetric, fallbackMetric, registeredAt }`. `formatId` is `question`, `contrast`, or `scenario`; metrics are `three-second-hold-rate`, `average-watch-ratio`, or YouTube Shorts’ `chose-to-view-rate`. Register the hypothesis before publishing so results are not explained after the fact.
- `ctaExperiment`: required immutable registration metadata after a CTA edit: `{ id, formatId, ruleVersion, offerId, keyword, hypothesis, changedVariable, comparisonGroupId, registeredAt, copyHash, acknowledgedCorrectionIds }`. `content/cta-lab.json` is the separate source of mandatory owner corrections and CTA learning; read it in every chat before CTA work. Each registration snapshots CTA wording, visual beats/transitions/placement, and current lesson, hook, and voice context. A script, visual, context, or placement change requires a new CTA version before voice preparation or render.
- `scenes[]`: ordered scene records with `id`, `kind`, `headline`, `narration`, optional concise `screenCaption`, `captionStyleId`, `editPlan`, optional `durationSec`/`durationFrames`, `visualBeats[]`, and Chinese `cards[]`. Scene edit directions follow `content/EDITING_SPEC.md`; headline/screenCaption remain planning metadata in action lessons and never create another visible text layer. Imported review scenes also carry audio-aligned `startMs`/`endMs`.
- `motionDesign:true`, a supported `motionDesignStyle`, and lesson-scene `motionBeats[]` select narrated motion graphics. The implemented `le-context-v1` treatment illustrates this lesson's book purchase, changed decision, and two positions of 了; do not reuse it for unrelated learning content. Each beat is `{id,kind,cueText,cueOccurrence?}`; supported kinds and exact-cue validation live in `scripts/motion-design-plan.mjs`. The optional occurrence disambiguates repeated words within the scene. Keep this visual plan separate from unchanged narration/captions and from the central CTA. Changes bind to a new immutable CTA context; legacy snapshots without this field remain unchanged. `motion-timeline.json` records the resolved word/frame anchors in the review folder.
- A visual beat may declare `visualFocus` (`friend`, `teacher`, `elder`, `shopkeeper`, `group`, `single-person`, `book`, `person-with-book`, `coffee`, `empty-cup`, `apple-near`, `apple-far`, or `apple-pair`) and `syncCueText` to switch a matching contextual illustration when that spoken word starts. Use only visuals supported by that scene's selected-language narration. CTA beats may also use `actionIcon` (`comment`, `app-download`, `direct-message`) and `transitionStyle` (`type-reveal`, `slide-match`, `mask-reveal`) inside the same central stage; keep them matched to the exact spoken word.
- Screenshot `screenHighlight` percentages refer to the intrinsic screenshot dimensions (`screenImageWidthPx` / `screenImageHeightPx`). The overlay follows the same contained image aspect ratio, including letterboxing; it must not target percentages of the surrounding padded card.
- Caption style IDs are `headline`, `pill`, or `highlight`. Use one style at a time per scene; legacy formats may use two or three treatments across the episode. Reusable action presentation follows `EDITING_SPEC.md`; a style name does not create an additional visible headline.
- `captions[]`: timed cue records `{ text, startMs, endMs, timestampMs, confidence }`, imported from word-level VTT/SRT.
- `captionSource`: new production uses `pending-gemini-generation` before voice work and `gemini-word-timestamps` after import. Older online TTS labels may remain in immutable historical snapshots.
- `audioSrc`, `voiceTextHash`, `voiceProfileId`: identify the imported audio and exact narration/profile version it belongs to. If narration changes, remove the hash/audio/captions and import a fresh pair.
- `audioTrimAfterFrames`, when present on an imported review episode, is Remotion's absolute source-frame endpoint (`Audio.trimAfter`), not a count of frames to remove from the tail. The importer sets it after the last timed word plus a short natural pause.
- `publishingStatus`: records pending confirmation, owner-approved Zernio publication, or the per-version outcome (`published`, `scheduled`, `processing`, `partial`, or `failed`).

## Platform CTA variants

For new lesson plans, omit top-level `cta`, `ctaExperiment` and the CTA scene. `scripts/episode-cta-assembly.mjs` can join a selected approved CTA ending to the shared lesson scenes; the normal production loader currently defaults to Instagram. Instagram and YouTube read the same canonical wording through `offer.canonicalRussian`; YouTube replaces only the two supplied screenshot frames in the final CTA scene. This is one shared lesson, not separately authored/generated lesson videos.

TikTok is not an authorized publishing destination. The normal make/render/MCP flow automatically creates a YouTube package from the shared working lesson and its approved ending. The shared lesson frames are rendered once; continuous audio is reused in both final exports. YouTube publishing is authorized after exact-preview approval. Each YouTube preview must capture title, visibility, made-for-kids selection, and synthetic-media disclosure. `scripts/platform-cta.mjs` prepares the selected CTA ending; `scripts/platform-exports.mjs` verifies the identical shared lesson/narration/timing and binds a matching immutable registration. A derived baseline inherits only the base’s explicitly acknowledged current corrections. Changed narration or an unacknowledged correction blocks automatic reuse. A visual-only CTA change still needs its own immutable CTA registration and final render/review; when spoken text and cue timing are unchanged, reuse the shared voice and word-level captions. No unapproved draft may render. Do not rewrite legacy approved or published snapshots.

## Hook measurement record

- `content/hook-lab.json` stores the initial comparison rules and owner-entered 24h/7d platform analytics snapshots. YouTube’s `chose-to-view-rate` readback uses the dashboard’s “How many chose to view” percentage.
- Readbacks are grouped by hook version, platform, time window, and the same primary metric. Do not compare platform totals as if their view definitions were identical.
- The 28 September package is a baseline candidate and has no platform result until the owner posts it and enters dashboard metrics.
- Hook rules are recommendations until enough comparable readbacks exist and the owner chooses to promote a pattern.

## Review package

Publication previews store a `publicationIntent` with `mode`, calendar `timezone` and selected `platforms`. A scheduled target may carry its own offset-qualified `scheduledFor` for an account-specific time; the top-level `scheduledFor` remains the fallback for historical shared-time previews. Every selected target must resolve to a future timestamp. Publish-now previews cannot contain either schedule field. Returned Zernio times are checked against the selected target's approved instant.

Each language writes its own review root: the original at `review/<id>/`, and the translated series at `review/<id>/tg/`. Within that root:

- `episode.json` — mutable working copy of the canonical scenario.
- `tts-input.txt`, `gemini-production/<timestamp>/` — exact narration, API WAV/MP3, word-level VTT, transcript diagnostics and generation metadata. The importer copies the approved MP3 into `public/generated/`.
- `video.mp4` — 1080×1920 Remotion export with captions burned in.
- `captions.srt` — sidecar made from the same timed cues.
- `post.md` — selected-language post copy and CTA.
- `status.json` — current package stage, duration, voice profile, subtitle source and `platformPackages` map. Each platform entry stores exact episode/video/SRT/post paths, `baseContentHash`, platform `contentHash`, `videoSha256`, `ctaExperimentId`, `ctaCopyHash` and `ctaRuleVersion`.
- `youtube/episode.json`, `youtube/video.mp4`, `youtube/captions.srt`, `youtube/post.md` — derived YouTube review output with its approved final CTA; the common lesson and imported speech/cues remain identical. The root `video.mp4` is the Instagram export.
- `.render-work/` — temporary shared lesson, CTA segments and continuous audio mix; removed after successful exports.
- `status.json` also stores the preregistered hook format/version and its honest performance state (`unmeasured` until dashboard data is logged).
- `approval.json` — approval of the exact Zernio preview: content hash, platform accounts, captions, platform settings, and immediate/scheduled time. Its `platformPackages` map binds each selected target to the exact exported file, media hash, content hash and CTA version. Exact-version approval archives are written separately for each selected CTA. Approval records consent; it does not upload or publish by itself.
- `publications.json` — per-content-version record of Zernio post IDs, platforms/accounts, status, scheduled time, and returned links. Each platform result also preserves its approved video/media hash, content hash and CTA experiment ID. Per-post status is updated as Zernio moves a post from scheduled or processing to published; records are retained across re-renders.

## Edit and invalidation rules

- Edit dated working copies, never the canonical plan, unless the owner asks to change future plans.
- A narration edit changes the narration fingerprint. The old audio, timestamps, MP4, SRT, post, and package approval are invalid.
- A visual or packaging edit can reuse audio and timestamps, but invalidates both current platform exports and package approval until re-rendered. Immutable approval archives and publication records remain intact.
- Motion, sound, caption grouping, typography, and safe-area defaults come from `content/visual-system.json` and `content/EDITING_SPEC.md`. Any renderer-level change requires rebuilding and reviewing the dated package.
- Source receipts may be added through MCP plan edits. They do not replace a human check of the claims.
